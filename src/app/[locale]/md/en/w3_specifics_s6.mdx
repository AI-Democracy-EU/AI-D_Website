For fairer hiring outcomes, companies must understand how these algorithms function, especially as opaque models may introduce unintentional biases without scrutiny. There is a need for transparency.

An inappropriate or biased data basis can lead to the AI making decisions that unfairly disadvantage certain groups of applicants. It is therefore of utmost importance to critically review the data sources and training methods and ensure that they are free from bias ([IQB Career Services, 2023](../bibliography#w3-4)). A striking example of this is the discrimination against women based on their gender in AI-supported evaluation processes, such as those that have occurred at Amazon ([Wilke 2018](../bibliography#w3-10)). This underlines the need to recognize possible biases and inequalities in the use of AI for ranking purposes.

To do this, it is necessary to collect data that is free of bias or to remove bias from the data. In addition, the data needs to be regularly reviewed and updated through updates and the critical use of AI support systems in HR.

Intentional, inclusive algorithm design can combine hiring efficiency with ethical, equitable outcomes, helping companies build dynamic and diverse teams without sacrificing speed.
