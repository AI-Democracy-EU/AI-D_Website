To understand, interpret, and reflect on the output of GenAI, it is important to consider the probabilities according to which LLMs form new patterns. They are based on the already existing human-made data and may reflect and reproduce racism, sexism, capitalism, etc. This is because the historical data from (digital) archives can be biased and thus teach stereotypes and majority-centred information to LLMs. The statistics derived from the data form the foundation of AI models. Based on the statistics, LLMs can recognize patterns and reproduce them. However, they are also limited and cause some flawed outputs. This is because LLMs cannot differentiate correlations and probabilities from casualties which in turn may lead to “findings [that] suggest a remarkably strong association between the popularity of the name Tyler and gasoline consumption in Italy” ([Tyler Vigen, Spurious Correlations #1,080](../bibliography#w2-14)). GenAI therefore does not calculate facts or reflect knowledge but rather calculates statistical correlations. Understanding and making sense of AI-generated content remains a human task.
