GenAI has sparked a significant debate regarding its impact on cultural productions and the arts. One of the primary concerns is that GenAI models are often trained on vast datasets that include the works of real artists, writers, and creators, frequently without their explicit consent or knowledge. This practice raises pressing ethical questions, as it essentially means that creatives are involuntarily contributing their intellectual property and artistic expressions to commercial AI endeavours. Furthermore, there is a growing apprehension that the widespread adoption of GenAI in creative industries could potentially compromise the quality and originality of cultural works. As companies increasingly prioritise efficiency and profit margins, there's a risk that the nuanced, human touch in art and cultural productions may be sacrificed in favour of AI-generated content. This shift not only challenges the traditional notions of creativity and authorship but also threatens to reshape the landscape of cultural production in ways that may devalue the unique contributions of human artists and creators.
