The integration of GenAI in education is poised to change teaching and learning methodologies across all levels of schooling. As AI-powered tools become more prevalent, traditional classroom dynamics are likely to shift, with personalised learning experiences tailored to individual student needs becoming increasingly common. GenAI could assist teachers in creating customised lesson plans, generating diverse educational content, and providing instant feedback on student work. However, this technological advancement also necessitates a fundamental change in curriculum design, with a greater emphasis on developing critical thinking skills, creativity, and emotional intelligence - areas where humans still outperform AI. The ability to critically evaluate AI-generated content and understand its limitations becomes paramount, making AI literacy a crucial competence across all disciplines. Students and teachers will need to learn not just how to use AI tools effectively, but also how to discern their biases, limitations, and potential ethical implications. As such, educators face the challenge of integrating AI literacy into their teaching while simultaneously adapting to the new possibilities and challenges presented by GenAI in the classroom. This shift underscores the importance of continuous professional development for teachers and the need for educational policies that address the ethical use of AI in learning environments.
