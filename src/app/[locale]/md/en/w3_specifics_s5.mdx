With hundreds of applications per position, companies use algorithms to streamline hiring, making the process faster and less labor-intensive across stages from job posting to interview selection.

### Algorithmic Screening Tools:

Filtering algorithms in recruiting platforms select applicants based on traits that recruiters consider ideal, though these filters are often subjective and may exclude qualified candidates.

Applicants may undergo tests or submit resumes that algorithms score, and rank based on pre-set criteria, advancing only top-scoring candidates.

### Limitations of Filtering Tools:

Filters are frequently based on recruiter intuition rather than proven predictors of job success, leading to inefficiencies and possible over-filtering of potentially successful applicants.

Standard algorithms assess candidates based on data from prior hires, promoting individuals who align with previous "successful" hires, but risk reinforcing outdated biases.

### Bias in Machine Learning:

As standard algorithms replicate past successes, they often overlook diverse applicants and may perpetuate cycles of biased decision-making that do not necessarily reflect current talent needs.

### Exploratory Algorithms for Inclusion:

Inclusive algorithms blend data-driven scoring with openness to unique candidates, broadening the talent pool by including those who deviate from traditional hiring patterns.

When used effectively, exploratory algorithms enhance workforce diversity and may reveal high-performing candidates from underrepresented groups or non-traditional backgrounds.
