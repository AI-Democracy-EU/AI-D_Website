In contrast to search engines, which only reproduce existing content, LLMs can generate new content by combining existing data. However, GenAI is not creative in the way humans are but can be used for creative purposes by humans. The way GenAI works can lead to mistakes or "hallucinations" ([Bastian 2024](../bibliography#w2-1)). Hallucinations are outputs that seem plausible because they are allegedly backed up by the training data but are objectively wrong. For example, ChatGPT might wrongly claim that Vincent van Gogh painted the Mona Lisa ([Funke 2024](../bibliography#w2-5)) or suggest books for research purposes that do not exist. For factual questions, it is therefore better to use reliable sources or knowledge overviews and not uncritically accept the answer provided by an LLM. There are already some AI-powered tools that provide answers based on verified web sources and include references with the response which helps users to engage with the results of GenAI critically. Nevertheless, a critical reflection of the answer detached from the information given by an AI model is still important.
