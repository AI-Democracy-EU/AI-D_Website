### The EU AI Act
-	In 2024, the European Union/European Parliament and the Council of the EU passed a law that addresses risks specifically created by AI applications – the EU AI Act.
-	AI Applications are ranked according to their risk level. Depending on their risk level, different rules apply.
-	For example, AI systems with unacceptable risk, like the classification of people based on their socio-economic status, real-time surveillance with biometrics, exploitation of vulnerabilities or behaviour manipulation, are forbidden.
-	On the contrary, regulations of AI with limited risks, like generative AI, are made by different bodies (e.g. a Ministry, a university, or a publishing house) and determine how to credit AI-generated content and publish summaries of copyrighted data used for training.
-	The AI Act applies to public and private actors within and outside the EU. When an AI system is on the EU market or affects people located in the EU, the rules apply.

The AI Act is a regulatory framework, but it’s still open to how these laws can be enforced in practice. This pressing question must be answered transparently and through the active involvement of civil society.
