Because of its fast and disruptive development, the use of GenAI is often unreflective and even problematic. Consequently, policymakers and tech companies face mounting pressure to develop robust detection methods and regulatory frameworks to combat the malicious use of GenAI in all fields and safeguard the foundations of democratic societies. As with other new and far-reaching technologies, a critical and public engagement with GenAI can be understood as an act of citizenship. The training of critical AI skills across disciplines also includes engaging with critical questions that touch on ethical, legal, and economic issues of (Gen)AI. This incomplete list should be considered:

- Who trains LLMs and under what conditions?
- What is the source of the training data?
- Is the tool I used based on an open or closed access model?
- Is it legitimate, that AI harvests all kinds of data on the web to train their data models?
- Is it legitimate, that companies exploit artistic and intellectual content to train their models and generate economic surplus from them?
- Were content creators remunerated?
- What kind of biases are reproduced by the data?
- Who has the resources for AI? Who is likely to benefit from them?
- Is my individual and personalized data secure when using a specific AI tool?
