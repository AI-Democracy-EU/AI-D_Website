Data protection and political regulation issues arise for applicants: They should be able to decide whether they want to be assessed by AI. The AI's criteria must be transparent and there should be opportunities to challenge incorrect decisions. AI-based HR (and hiring as part of it) is high risk according to the EU AI Act.

### AI in data protection in the European Union

In the European Union companies are obliged by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) to provide transparent information about how personal data is processed. This usually includes information on what data is collected, how it is used and how long it is stored. When AI or automated decision-making systems are used, especially if these decisions have legal effects or significantly affect the data subject, companies must state this in their privacy policy (GDPR).

However, for reasons of transparency (Art. 5 para. 1 letter a GDPR), the use of AI should already be mentioned in the job advertisement. If a fully automated rejection is made to applicants based on an AI application, this is an automated individual decision that is prohibited under Art. 22 GDPR. If, on the other hand, the employer ultimately makes the decision which applicants will be invited to an interview based on a ranking by a system, the requirements of Art. 22 GDPR are not met ([Deichmann+Fuchs](../bibliography#w3-2)).
