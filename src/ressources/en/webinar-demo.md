### Chapter 1: Fundamentals of AI

#### 1. What is Artificial Intelligence (AI), and how does it work?

![Seven people and a dog illustrated in watercolour](/md-images/Webinar_1_Page_03_Image.jpg)

What is AI?

AI is a field of computer science that creates systems to generate recommendations, texts, images, or sounds based on human interactions.

To key terminologies are:
- Autonomy: The ability to perform tasks in complex environments without constant guidance by a user.
- Adaptivity: The ability to improve performance by learning from experience.

The EU AI Act defines AI like this:

"'AI system' means a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments"[1].

That sounds pretty complicated, doesn't it? Let's break it down:

Part 1: AI Systems are machine based (e.g. computers, servers), have varying degrees of autonomy, and can adapt.

Part 2: AI Systems process data from the input they receive (e.g. your questions to an AI chatbot) to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments

#### Terms

There are a lot of terms floating around when we talk about AI. In the picture, you can see how they are connected. Nowadays, most AI systems (e.g., ChatGPT) adopt machine learning techniques. So often when we talk about AI, we are actually talking about machine learning.

Machine learning is "a type of artificial intelligence in which computers use huge amounts of data to learn how to do tasks rather than being programmed to do them"[2].

![Own illustration](/md-images/Webinar_1_Page_04_Image_0001.jpg)

Deep Learning is a type of AI that teaches computers to learn from large amounts of data by using layers of simple decision-making steps. It helps computers do things like recognise images, understand speech, and translate languages automatically, without needing humans to tell them what to look for.

AI systems are tools trained to repeat behaviours. AI can only imitate human behaviour. It's not intelligent the same way humans are. AI is a new form of automation that can repeat patterns based on statistical probabilities.

### Chapter 2: Potentials and Challenges of AI

#### 1. What are the potentials and challenges of AI?

You just saw a lot of examples of how AI is used. AI Systems can have a lot of benefits. You are probably using some applications already. Nevertheless, there are also some challenges with the new developments of AI. This chapter provides an overview of the potentials and the challenges that come with the use of AI applications.

#### 2. Potentials of AI:

AI can take over a lot of tasks with automation. It is possible to work more efficiently with the help of AI Systems. AI can also optimise processes, such as visualisation and analysis. That means AI Systems can simplify labour and automate repetitive tasks.

AI can provide decision-making support for complex tasks.

AI can help reduce disadvantages, e.g. as a tutor. Students have unique learning needs, and AI Systems can provide a learning experience designed to meet them.

![An illustration containing electronical devices that are connected by arm-like structures](/md-images/Webinar_1_Page_06_Image_0001.jpg)

#### 3. Challenges of AI:

Although the Potentials of AI are great, they come with certain risks.

Privacy and Security issues can arise. AI Systems collect and use large amounts of data to achieve the best results. For example, assistants like Alexa gather audio information. Another example would be facial recognition applications.

If AI Systems are used for automated decision-making, questions on human autonomy and agency arise. AI Systems have started to impact humans' ability to act and make decisions. For example, some companies begin to implement automated recruitment software. Sometimes, these systems work with biased training data, which can lead to unfair conditions in the recruitment process.

Generally biased training data can amplify injustices and discrimination.

A big challenge with AI Systems is the possible loss of human touch and social interaction. AI-Tutoring systems, for example can work perfectly fine for some tasks, but they lack skills that a real teacher has, like empathy or humour.

### Chapter 3: Democratic Regulation of AI: The EU AI Act

#### 1. How do politics and democracy react to AI challenges? How do they ensure that AI is used best?

You just learned that, even though AI has a lot of potential and positive aspects, risks must be managed. Political decision-makers try to come up with rules for governments and private companies to ensure that AI is used in a responsible way.

Political regulation of AI ensures:
- Democratic control
- Transparency, Accountability, Inclusiveness, and respect for Human Rights
- No misuse of users' private and sensitive data

#### 2. The EU AI Act

In 2024, the European Union/European Parliament and the Council of the EU passed a law that addresses risks specifically created by AI applications – the EU AI Act[3].

AI Applications are ranked according to their risk level. Depending on their risk level, different rules apply. For example, AI systems with unacceptable risk, like the classification of people based on their socio-economic status, are forbidden.

On the contrary, regulations of AI with limited risk, like ChatGPT, are made by different bodies (e.g. a Ministry, a university, a publishing house) and determine how to credit AI-generated content and publish summaries of copyrighted data used for training.

The AI Act applies to public and private actors within and outside the EU. When an AI system is on the EU market or affects people located in the EU, the rules apply.
